ICE-PATH: Lunar Ice Detection & Rover Traverse Planning (ISRO BAH 2026)

Jun 2026 – Jul 2026


Associated with Sahyadri College of Engineering & Management

Collaborated with Team LunarKinematics for ISRO’s Bharatiya Antariksh Hackathon (BAH) 2026 (Challenge 08). The project focused on detecting subsurface water ice in the Moon’s permanently shadowed south-polar craters using Chandrayaan-2 data, and translating that detection into an actionable landing-site and rover-traverse plan.
System Overview:
• Ice Confidence Index: Fused dual-frequency (L/S-band) DFSAR radar with terrain and illumination models, requiring both radar bands to agree before flagging ice to reject single-band false positives.
• Site Selection: Ranked candidate landing sites based on slope, hazards, illumination, ice potential, and ground communication visibility.
• Path Planning: Integrated a weighted A* search algorithm for rover traverse planning with dynamic profiles (Safety-First vs. Shortest-Path) that recalculate in real-time based on site selection.
My Contributions:
• Acted as a core cross-functional team member, collaborating on the conceptualization and logic design of the Ice Confidence Index and rover navigation constraints.
• Contributed to the research and analysis of Chandrayaan-2 lunar terrain and radar data to help validate the accuracy of our hazard and ice-detection models.
• Assisted in system integration and testing, ensuring the A* path planning algorithms interfaced smoothly with the data pipeline and provided reliable, real-time traverse calculations.

Skills: Algorithm Design, Data Fusion, +5 skills
